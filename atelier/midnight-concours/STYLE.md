# Midnight Concours — Style Prompt

> A black studio, a sculpted form, and long strips of light that slide over it. The shape is never shown whole at first: it is described by reflections travelling along its surfaces.
> References (grammar only): automotive studio photography with strip lights; clay design models; the reveal under a silk cover at a concours. Never copy a real car's design, a marque's grille, badge or lamp signature.

Technique base: `glass-product` (reflections, studio lighting, depth of field), `hologram-hud` (fine technical overlays, used sparingly). Atelier style: no demo film yet, see [`../README.md`](../README.md).

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

Design told through highlights. A form with long, taut surfaces sits in darkness, and bands of light move across it so that the eye assembles the shape from the way reflections bend, pinch and release. It suits anything with a designed surface: a car, a motorcycle, a watch case, a speaker, a tool. The subject need not be a vehicle, and it should be an invented form.

Not a showroom turntable, not a racing film (nothing is fast until the last shot), not a wireframe.

## 2. Materials and rendering

- **Paint**: deep, with a clear coat. A sharp reflection on top and a softer coloured one beneath. Dark colours work best: black, midnight blue, deep green, burgundy.
- **Light strips**: long rectangular sources overhead and at the sides, seen only as reflections. Their edges are crisp, and they taper where the surface curves away.
- **Floor**: black and glossy, with a dim mirror image of the subject and the strips.
- **Details**: polished metal, dark glass, a lamp that lights with a slow fade. Each gets one close shot.
- **Form**: build surfaces from smooth curves with deliberate crease lines. Reflections expose every flaw, so keep geometry clean and curvature continuous.
- **Overlay**, optional and rare: a hairline dimension or a single figure in small tracked capitals.

## 3. Colour logic

Black studio, one body colour, white light. A single warm or red accent for a lamp or a stitched detail. Lights are neutral white, and a slightly warm strip may be used once for the hero shot.

## 4. Type

Small, wide-tracked capitals in a clean grotesque, white at 80 %, low in the frame. A model name or a single number may be set large and thin. Nothing italic, nothing condensed.

## 5. Motion and camera

- **The light moves first.** A strip travels the length of the form in 3 to 4 s while the camera holds.
- Camera moves are low, slow and parallel to the surface: a track along the flank, a rise over a shoulder line.
- Start in close detail and withhold the full shape until two thirds of the way through.
- One change of pace at the end is allowed: lamps on, a short push-in, cut to black.

## 6. Sound

A low sustained synth or bowed bass with slow harmonic movement. Under it, studio quiet. Foley: the soft electrical hum of lights coming up, a latch, a cover sliding off. A single deep note on the full reveal. Tempo around 60 bpm, felt more than heard.

## 7. Native moves

- **The light sweep**: darkness, then one strip runs along a crease and the line of the whole design is drawn in a single reflection.
- **The cover**: cloth pulled back along the length of the form, reflections appearing behind it.
- **Detail, detail, whole**: three tight shots of surfaces, then the wide shot the film has been holding back.
- **Lamps on**: the subject lights itself and is, for the first time, a source.

## 8. Pitfalls

- Lighting the object instead of lighting what it reflects. Use large sources and a dark room.
- Faceted or lumpy surfaces. Reflections will wobble; smooth the geometry.
- Showing the whole shape in the first shot.
- Overlays everywhere. One figure, once.
