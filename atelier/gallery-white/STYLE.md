# Gallery White — Style Prompt

> A museum after closing. White walls, pale floor, daylight from above, and each idea of the film standing on its own plinth with a small label beside it.
> References (grammar only): contemporary gallery interiors; museum vitrines and wall labels; architectural models in white card. Never copy a real museum's rooms, a known artwork or an institution's label design.

Technique base: `brick-toy` (small 3D objects under soft studio light), `tilt-shift` (miniature depth of field and quiet camera). Atelier style: no demo film yet, see [`../README.md`](../README.md).

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

The film is a walk through an exhibition that was built for the user's subject. Each point becomes an exhibit: an object on a plinth, a model under glass, a framed sheet, a row of specimens. The room gives every exhibit the same respect, and the labels do the explaining in very few words.

Not a virtual museum tour with avatars, not a white-background product shot, not an infographic. The exhibits are objects, and they cast shadows.

## 2. Materials and rendering

- **Walls and plinths**: matte white with a trace of warmth, never pure white; a soft contact shadow where each plinth meets the floor.
- **Floor**: pale oak or polished concrete, faintly reflective, its lines leading into the room.
- **Light**: a skylight. Soft, from above, with gentle gradients on the walls and a slightly brighter pool on each exhibit. Shadows are soft-edged and short.
- **Exhibits**: simply modelled, in honest materials: plaster, card, brass, glass, wood, one coloured object per room. Vitrines have thin glass with a single reflection.
- **Labels**: a small white card, bottom right of the exhibit, mounted slightly off the wall so it casts a hairline shadow.
- **Scale**: figures are rarely shown. When one is, it is small, still and seen from behind.

## 3. Colour logic

White, pale wood and shadow grey carry the film. Each room has one coloured exhibit, and that colour is the room's subject. The wall behind a key exhibit may be painted a deep muted tone (museum green, oxblood, slate) once in the film.

## 4. Type

Label typography: a clear grotesque (Inter or Jost) in dark grey, with a title line, a line of small capitals for date or category, and at most two lines of text. Room titles are vinyl lettering on the wall, large, in a serif, read as the camera passes.

## 5. Motion and camera

- A slow walk at eye level, straight along the room's axis, with stops. Or a track sideways along a wall of frames.
- At each exhibit: arrive, hold 2 s, push in slightly as the label fades up, hold, move on.
- Exhibits may do one thing while watched: turn a quarter, open, light up, assemble. Then they are still again.
- Doorways are the cuts: the camera passes through an opening into the next room and the next chapter.

## 6. Sound

A large quiet room: soft footsteps, air, a distant door. A sparse piano or a string quartet, as if playing in another gallery. A small tone when each label appears. Narration, if any, in the calm voice of an audio guide.

## 7. Native moves

- **The enfilade**: several doorways in line, each room a chapter, seen at once before the walk begins.
- **Under glass**: a vitrine in which a model builds itself while the camera circles a quarter turn.
- **The salon wall**: many small frames; the camera finds one and it fills the screen.
- **Lights out**: the skylight dims at the end, and one exhibit stays lit.

## 8. Pitfalls

- Blown-out white with no gradients. The room needs soft shading to have volume.
- Crowding. One exhibit per view, with wall around it.
- Labels that become paragraphs.
- A fast or floating camera. It should feel like a person walking.
