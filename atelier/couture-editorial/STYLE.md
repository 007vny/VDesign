# Couture Editorial — Style Prompt

> A fashion monthly that moves. Large high-contrast serif type, a great deal of white, and one picture at a time, placed as an art director would place it on a spread.
> References (grammar only): mid-century fashion magazine layouts; the modern Didone title page; contact sheets and crop marks. Never copy a magazine's masthead, a cover layout, a photographer's image or a commercial typeface.

Technique base: `swiss-motion` (grid, type as the moving element), `spy-titles` (cut-out silhouettes and bold type choreography). Atelier style: no demo film yet, see [`../README.md`](../README.md).

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

Each shot is a spread. Type is the main image: a word set so large it is cropped by the page, with a hairline against a thick stem. Pictures are few and treated as objects: a framed rectangle, a cut-out figure, a strip of contact prints. The rhythm comes from turning pages and from scale changes between enormous and tiny.

Not a slideshow of photographs, not Swiss neutrality (this style has attitude and contrast), not a social media template.

## 2. Materials and rendering

- **Paper**: warm white `#f6f2ea` with faint fibre and a soft shadow at the gutter. Occasionally a full black page.
- **Type**: a Didone with real hairlines (Bodoni Moda, Playfair Display). Hairlines must stay at least 2 px at the output size, so set large.
- **Pictures**: drawn or composed in code as tonal images with a photographic feel (soft grain, a limited tonal range), or the user's own photographs. Each has a thin keyline or a white border, and sits on the grid.
- **Figures**: when no photograph exists, elongated cut-paper silhouettes, about nine heads tall, in one flat colour with a single fold shadow.
- **Print marks**: crop marks, a page number, a small running title. Sparingly, as texture.

## 3. Colour logic

Black, paper white, and one colour per film used at full strength (a red, a cobalt, a saffron or a pink). The colour appears as one block, one word or one garment per spread, never as a tint over everything.

## 4. Type

Three sizes only: enormous (a word fills the width), text (a short standfirst in italic), and tiny (tracked capitals in a plain sans for credits and captions). Mixed case for titles; italics for emphasis; never bold sans headlines.

## 5. Motion and camera

- **Page turns and hard cuts.** A turn takes 0.5 s; the next spread is already composed when it lands.
- Type moves in straight lines along the baseline or the stem, slowly, like a headline being nudged into place. A letter may scale from tiny to enormous as a transition.
- Pictures slide on the grid, or are revealed by a mask in the shape of a letter.
- The camera never moves in depth. This is a flat world with one exception: a slight lift and shadow when a page turns.
- Pace alternates: three quick spreads (1 to 1.5 s), then one held for 4 s.

## 6. Sound

A dry, confident rhythm: brushed drums or a plucked bass, with a single piano or organ stab on title spreads. Paper sounds are the foley: a page turned, a sheet slid, scissors once. Leave bars of near silence on the held spreads.

## 7. Native moves

- **The cropped word**: one word too big for the page, sliding until the meaningful part is in frame.
- **Letter as window**: a counter or stem opens and the picture is inside it.
- **Contact sheet**: a strip of frames runs past; one is circled and becomes the next spread.
- **The colour page**: a full bleed of the film's colour with one line of small white type.

## 8. Pitfalls

- Hairlines that vanish or shimmer. Check at output size, and keep type large.
- Filling the white. If a spread feels empty, it is probably right.
- More than one colour, or the colour used as a background wash.
- Centring everything. Editorial layout is asymmetrical and anchored to a grid.
