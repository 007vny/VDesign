# Champagne Hour — Style Prompt

> The last hour of daylight, seen through a glass. Warm haze, soft discs of out-of-focus light, and a fine bead of bubbles rising through every shot.
> References (grammar only): golden-hour photography with a fast lens; a coupe on a terrace; fairy lights far behind the subject. Never copy a wine house's label, bottle shape, crest or advertising film.

Technique base: `firefly-pointillism` (pictures built from points of light), `glass-product` (glass, liquid, refraction). Atelier style: no demo film yet, see [`../README.md`](../README.md).

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

A film made of light more than of objects. The background is always out of focus: discs of warm light that drift and overlap. In front of it, one thing is sharp: a glass, a hand, a ring, a word. Bubbles rise in thin, straight strings from fixed points, and their speed sets the pace.

Not glitter or sparkle effects, not lens flares, not a party montage. It is quiet, and the celebration is implied.

## 2. Materials and rendering

- **Bokeh**: discs with a slightly brighter rim and a soft interior, sizes following depth, additive where they overlap. A few are cut by the lens edge into cat's-eye shapes near the corners.
- **Haze**: a warm gradient from the light source, lifting the blacks on that side only.
- **Glass**: thin, with two highlights (window and rim) and the background refracted upside down and compressed inside it.
- **Liquid**: pale gold, brighter than its surroundings because light passes through it.
- **Bubbles**: tiny, 2 to 5 px, each a ring with one bright point. They leave from a few nucleation points in strings, accelerate slightly, wobble by a pixel, and gather at the surface.
- **Grain**: fine and warm, 4 %.

## 3. Colour logic

A single warm family: deep amber shadows, gold mids, cream highlights. One cool note is allowed far in the background (the blue of dusk) to make the warmth read. No saturated colours.

## 4. Type

A light serif italic in cream (Cormorant Garamond Italic), set small and placed in the sharp plane. Words come into focus rather than fading in: they start as blur discs and resolve. Dates and names only; this style does not explain.

## 5. Motion and camera

- **Focus is the verb.** Reveals are focus pulls: 1.2 to 2 s, with the background discs growing as the foreground sharpens.
- The camera drifts a few pixels, as if handheld by someone standing very still.
- Bubbles rise at a constant rate throughout the film, across cuts. Their rate is the tempo.
- Shots of 4 to 7 s, dissolves of about 1 s. Few hard cuts.

## 6. Sound

A felt piano, a soft upright bass, perhaps a brushed snare far away. Room sound of an evening outdoors: distant voices with no words, a little wind. Foley: the fine hiss of bubbles close to the microphone, glass touching glass once. Warm and quiet.

## 7. Native moves

- **Resolve**: the whole frame is discs of light; one of them sharpens into the subject.
- **Through the glass**: the camera looks through the liquid, the scene behind inverted and golden, then pulls focus to the scene itself.
- **The bead**: follow one string of bubbles upward to the surface, where the next shot begins.
- **The toast**: two rims meet; the highlight jumps, the sound rings, and the title resolves in the hold after it.

## 8. Pitfalls

- Sharp backgrounds. If the background is readable, the style is gone.
- Bubbles that swirl or drift like snow. They go up in lines.
- Orange. Keep to gold and amber; push it too far and it becomes a sunset filter.
- Sparkle sprites with four points. Highlights are discs.
