# Haute Horlogerie — Style Prompt

> The film takes place inside a mechanical movement. Bridges are landscapes, jewels are lamps, and the balance wheel keeps the tempo of the edit.
> References (grammar only): macro photography of watch movements; watchmakers' exploded drawings; the loupe's narrow plane of focus. Never copy a real calibre's layout, a brand's bridge shapes, engraving or logo.

Technique base: `precise-mechanism` (gear trains that really mesh, colour-coded parts), `glass-product` (metal, sapphire, depth of field). Atelier style: no demo film yet, see [`../README.md`](../README.md).

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

Everything on screen is a working part, and every motion has a cause the viewer can trace: the barrel turns the train, the train feeds the escapement, the escapement lets the balance swing. The pleasure is seeing that nothing is decorative. A story is told by following energy through the mechanism, with each stage standing for a stage of the user's subject.

Not a spinning 3D logo, not steampunk (no brass pipes, no rust, no rivets), not a technical diagram (surfaces are finished metal under real light).

## 2. Materials and rendering

- **Steel and rhodium bridges** with parallel stripes (Geneva stripes) that catch the light in bands as the camera moves; edges are bevelled and polished, so every outline has a bright hairline.
- **Circular graining** (overlapping small circles) on the plate below.
- **Jewels**: synthetic ruby bearings, translucent crimson with a small hot highlight, set in polished sinks.
- **Screws**: heat-blued steel, a deep blue-violet, slot aligned by eye, never perfectly.
- **Gears are real**: tooth counts and ratios are computed, teeth mesh, and speeds follow from the ratios. A wheel never spins freely.
- **Lens**: macro, with a very shallow plane of focus that is racked from part to part.

## 3. Colour logic

Silver-grey metal on a near-black ground. Three small colours only, each with a meaning: ruby for bearings, blued steel for screws and hands, gold for the train wheels. If the user's subject needs an accent, it replaces the gold.

## 4. Type

Engraved, not overlaid: capitals in a narrow grotesque, cut into a bridge and filled with dark lacquer, lit by the same light as the metal. A caption that cannot be engraved is set small in ivory below the action, tracked wide. Numerals look like a dial's.

## 5. Motion and camera

- **The balance is the metronome.** Choose its frequency first (3 Hz reads well at 24 fps) and place cuts and title beats on multiples of its swing.
- Camera glides parallel to the plate, or racks focus between two depths. One slow pull-back at the end shows the whole movement.
- The escapement is shown at true speed once, then at a quarter speed so the tick can be read.
- Assembly shots (a bridge lowered, a screw turned home) move at the pace of a hand: 1.5 to 2.5 s each.

## 6. Sound

The tick is the rhythm section, recorded close, with its faint metallic ring. Under it, a slow string pad or celesta. Foley: tweezers on steel, a screw seating, a mainspring's soft tension. Tempo is the balance frequency divided down. No drums.

## 7. Native moves

- **Follow the power**: one continuous shot from barrel to balance, each wheel handing motion to the next.
- **The rack focus reveal**: a blurred shape behind a bridge comes into focus as the answer.
- **Exploded and returned**: parts lift apart along one axis in order, hold, and settle back until the movement runs again.
- **The first tick**: silence, the balance is released, and the whole frame starts moving.

## 8. Pitfalls

- Gears that overlap without meshing, or all turn at one speed. People notice at once.
- Lighting everything evenly. The stripes need a moving highlight to exist.
- Too many jewels and blued screws. Count them as a watchmaker would.
- Text floating in front of the mechanism in a bright colour.
