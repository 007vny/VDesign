# Marble & Brass — Style Prompt

> Slabs of veined stone, and thin brass inlay that draws itself across them to make plans, maps, monograms and charts. A lobby floor that explains things.
> References (grammar only): inlaid stone floors and tabletops; terrazzo with metal dividers; an architect's plan cut into a threshold. Never copy a real building's floor, a hotel's monogram or a patterned brand surface.

Technique base: `art-deco` (the metal line, symmetry, axial reveals), `blueprint` (plans and diagrams that draw themselves). Atelier style: no demo film yet, see [`../README.md`](../README.md).

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

The picture is a flat surface of stone seen from above or at a low raking angle. All drawing is done in brass: a line is routed into the stone, the metal fills it, and it is polished flush. Information is architecture here. A floor plan is a real plan, a timeline is a strip of inlay with stations, a chart is cut from contrasting stone.

Not a texture slideshow, not a 3D room tour (the camera stays with the surface), not Art Deco ornament for its own sake.

## 2. Materials and rendering

- **Stone**: procedural veining with depth: a soft base tone, broad cloudy variation, then fine branching veins that fade in and out of the surface. Two or three stones per film, for example a white with grey veins, a deep green, a near-black with gold veins.
- **Polish**: the surface reflects a soft window or skylight, visible only at raking angles; the reflection slides as the camera moves.
- **Brass inlay**: 3 to 6 px lines, flush with the stone, satin rather than mirror, with a narrow bright band that travels when the light does. Joints are mitred.
- **Cut stone shapes**: a second stone set into the first, with a hairline brass border. Used for bars in a chart, regions on a map, rooms in a plan.
- **Shadow**: almost none. Objects resting on the surface (a key, a cup, a model) cast one soft shadow.

## 3. Colour logic

The stones are the palette. Brass is the only line colour. One stone is the ground for the whole film; a second marks what matters; a third appears at most once.

## 4. Type

Inlaid brass capitals in a geometric sans with a little flare (Jost or Poiret One), tracked wide, sized like signage. Small text is engraved and filled dark. Numbers are large and few.

## 5. Motion and camera

- A line is routed, then filled: a dark groove advances, and the brass follows half a second behind.
- The camera travels over the surface like someone walking across a hall: steady, straight, turning at right angles. A tilt from top view to raking view reveals the polish.
- Stone shapes arrive by sliding in from an edge and seating with a small settle, or by a wipe of polish.
- Symmetry is the default. Reveals open from a centre line.

## 6. Sound

Footsteps in a large quiet room, far off. A cello and a piano, slow. Foley: a chisel's fine scrape for routing, a soft metallic ring as inlay seats, stone on stone. Long reverb, low level.

## 7. Native moves

- **The plan draws itself**, room by room, and the camera follows the route a visitor would take.
- **The threshold**: a brass strip crosses the frame; beyond it the stone changes, and so does the chapter.
- **The compass rose**: a centred inlay that turns into a dial, a chart or a clock.
- **Raking reveal**: from above the surface is plain; the camera tilts, and reflected light shows what was inlaid.

## 8. Pitfalls

- Veins that tile or repeat. Vary scale and let large areas stay quiet.
- Brass that looks like yellow paint: it needs the moving highlight and a darker edge.
- Too many stones. Three is the limit.
- Leaving the surface to fly through space. The discipline is the floor.
